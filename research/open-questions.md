# Open questions

*Proposed investigations into what could make engineering substantially more effective*

These questions are an agenda for inquiry. None of the investigations below has been run for this collection. The linked research informs the questions; it does not supply results for the proposed practices. Findings should record the setting, comparison, costs and counterexamples so that another team can judge what applies to its own work.

## Can exceptions reveal a useful next investment?

The proposal in [Engineering by exception](../essays/engineering-by-exception.md) is that recurring human intervention can reveal missing capabilities, unnecessary work or decisions that should remain with people.

Choose a recurring class of work and observe both interrupted and apparently successful cases. Before changing the workflow, classify the proposed explanation for each intervention and record what would distinguish it from alternatives. Compare that choice of investment with the team's existing improvement priorities. Follow the total work after the change, including rejected requests, support effort, repair and maintenance.

Evidence against the proposal would include expensive analysis that repeatedly recommends what the team already knew, or fewer interruptions accompanied by more undetected errors. It would also count against the method if it consistently overlooked opportunities to eliminate the requests. An interruption record describes the workflow that exists; it may conceal the case for replacing it.

## Can a farm improve delivery without becoming another review queue?

[When the farm studies the factory](../essays/when-the-farm-studies-the-factory.md) proposes bounded investigations whose findings can change or cancel delivery work.

Try one investigation with a named decision, a review budget and competing explanations. Record which findings changed a decision, which were rejected and why. Compare the resulting work with a reasonably similar recent change, while noting differences that prevent a clean causal comparison. Include the specialist attention used by both investigation and delivery.

The proposal is weakened if the farm consumes more of the constrained person's time than it releases, mainly produces recommendations nobody uses, or cannot influence which work enters the factory. A useful failed investigation may rule out an attractive but unnecessary investment. Record the decision it changed rather than counting reports or suggestions.

## Does the first improvement help develop a different capability?

Repeated use of a useful tool does not establish that the organization became better at developing capabilities. Before a second investment begins, name what the earlier work is expected to contribute and how that contribution could be checked. Retain the costs of understanding, adapting and maintaining it.

The existing [second-capability pilot](./second-capability.md) supplies a proposed comparison and reporting method. Include an attempt by someone other than the original author. A shorter second ticket is weak evidence if the task was simpler or its engineer more experienced. A concrete adverse result would be an inherited assumption that makes the new capability slower to build or less reliable than starting again.

## Can human judgment improve while routine implementation is delegated?

Anthropic's [study of AI assistance and coding-skill formation](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills) found lower immediate comprehension scores in a small randomized study of developers learning an unfamiliar library. It did not establish long-term effects, and its comparisons among interaction styles were observational.

The practice proposed in [How engineers become engineers](../essays/how-engineers-become-engineers.md) deserves its own test. Select a judgment the work requires, such as recognizing when a retry can repeat an external effect. Compare supported practice involving predictions and experiments with the team's normal review-based learning. Later, use an unfamiliar case and ask the engineer to choose a useful check and explain its limits. Keep this developmental rather than using it to rank individuals.

The proposal would be weakened if people became better at rehearsed explanations without improving on unfamiliar cases, or if the practice required more expert time than the organization could sustain. Immediate recall, delayed understanding and independent judgment should be reported separately.

## How should we value work that was previously too expensive to attempt?

METR's [Task Substitution and Uplift](https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-08-task-substitution-and-uplift/) distinguishes improvements measured on the old task mix, improvements measured on the new mix, and gains in value. Its theoretical comparison depends on simplifying assumptions. The practical concern is direct: pricing newly affordable work at its old hypothetical cost can exaggerate the value gained, while measuring only old work can miss new opportunities.

Follow an investigation that the team would previously have declined. State the decision it might inform before starting. Record what changed because of its result: a product choice, an abandoned project, a reduced uncertainty or a newly feasible outcome. Report the cost of rejected investigations too. Use outcome evidence appropriate to that decision rather than converting every generated experiment into hours supposedly saved.

If the investigation produces interesting material but changes no decision, do not report its hypothetical manual cost as a productivity gain. It may have produced useful learning; explain what someone is now able to understand or do and leave the wider value claim open.

## What would change our view of 10×?

A plausible route needs to account for the whole class of valuable outcomes, including the cases excluded from automation. It also needs to survive maintenance, changed requirements and people leaving. A local success that depends on one expert quietly repairing every difficult result is a different finding from a capability other people can use.

The [SPACE framework](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-space-of-developer-productivity-theres-more-to-it-than-you-think/) explains why productivity cannot be represented adequately by one activity measure. For these investigations, report useful outcomes, total effort and cost, quality, elapsed time and the capability retained in whatever combination fits the question. Explain tradeoffs rather than forcing them into one score.

The ambition would become more credible through documented sequences in which an early improvement helps develop a later one, substantial classes of work change or disappear, and the organization can sustain the result. Repeatedly finding that integration, judgment or maintenance absorbs the gains would narrow the settings in which the ambition is plausible. Both kinds of finding belong in the publications.
